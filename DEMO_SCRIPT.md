In this presentation, I’ll explain the idea, walk through the experience for both backers and creators, and share the main implementation choices and current prototype boundaries.” |

People can browse campaigns without creating an account, search by topic, narrow results by category, and change the sort order. Here I’m searching for a shade-related campaign. The goal is to make discovery quick without hiding the details people need before they decide to support a message.” |

These details help explain what the creator is proposing and how the campaign is meant to reach people.” |

| 3:55–4:45 | Open Create. Show Basics, editable starter text, then Story and its source fields. | “Now I’ll show the creator experience. Campaign creation is organized into five steps: Basics, Story, Campaign and Ad Visuals, Reach, and Submit. A creator can begin with editable sample copy, then add a title, category, creator name, and summary. In the Story step, they can provide more context and add supporting sources. Drafts save on the device, so the creator can continue later. The starter content is sample text; the prototype does not send it to an AI service.” |
| 4:45–5:30 | Show the separate campaign cover and ad visual controls, placement preview, then the Reach fields and audience preview. | “The visual step keeps the campaign cover separate from the advertising creative, because they serve different purposes. | 5:30–6:05 | Review campaign details and policies, submit, then show the dashboard and review status. | “Before submission, the creator can review the campaign and confirm the community policies. After submitting, the campaign is marked In review. The creator dashboard and review timeline show that status. | 6:05–6:42 | Show Activity, saved campaigns, creator dashboard, or contribution history. | “Beyond the main flows, the prototype includes saved campaigns, activity notifications, campaign status, and contribution history. These features help a person return to campaigns they follow and help a creator understand what is happening with a submission. For this assignment, these records are stored on the device, which keeps the demonstration self-contained.” |
| 6:42–7:22 | Show the app screens, then a simple architecture slide or return to Home. | “The app is built with Expo and React Native. Campaigns, drafts, activity, and contribution records use shared TypeScript data types and a local persistence adapter. That lets the interface demonstrate the key product flows without a live backend. For a production version, the server should own campaign validation, user permissions, fee and payment calculations, review decisions, and reporting. A payment provider would handle real transactions, and distribution reports would distinguish spend, impressions, reach, and clicks.” |
| 7:22–7:50 | End card with Amplify and the prototype label. | “To summarize, Amplify brings campaign discovery, transparent planning, creator tools, and a review step into one mobile experience. The next step would be connecting a secure backend and validating the reach model with real campaign data. Thank you for watching.” |
